Elevate and Serve Others #JoelKallmanDay

October 14, 2026 · 5 min read
Adrian Png
Principal Solutions Architect @ Miracle and Owner @ Smartweaver Digital

Over the last few weeks, I have been travelling in Southeast Asia to participate in the recent Oracle APEX AI Roadshow 2026 ASEAN Edition. Unfortunately, with the travel and preparations, I did not have the time to prepare a technical blog post for this year's #JoelKallmanDay (more about this community-driven activity can be found here). I have never missed a post for this event, so rather than rush a half-baked technical post, I thought I'd right about the "community" that Joel Kallman help strengthened.

Oracle ACE Programme​

I want to start off by first thanking the Oracle ACE programme and community.

The Oracle programme managers and leaders, past and present. For tirelessly running and keeping this small and special community together. For providing some of us the opportunity to travel and reach out to others both far and near.

The members of the programme who have mentored, inspired, and continue to challenge me to be better everyday.

I am also grateful for the Oracle ACE members that I have had the privilege of mentoring. Let me start with the most recent.

Jing Khai Lim is a highly energized young man who sacrifices precious time off from his NSF (full-time National Service) duties to participate in tech conferences, workshops, and hackathon. He's currently an Oracle ACE Apprentice and working towards final recognition as an Oracle ACE Associate. Let's get there!

@Jing Khai, sorry to disappoint you, but I think I still hold the honour of being the first Singaporean to be an Oracle ACE. However, I think you still get to be known as the first Singaporean to be an Oracle ACE who still lives in Singapore. ;-)

Then there's Gayan Lokumanna, who first impressed me with the number of Oracle certifications he has, including the OCM. I'm still hoping that you get your "Pro" very soon!

@Gayan, I think the community in Sri Lanka has truly benefited from your passion to generate more user group activities, and inspiring more Sri Lankans to join the Oracle ACE programme.

Building the Community in Southeast Asia​

Next, I wanted to thank the people who made the roadshow a success.

  • The attendees in Jakarta, Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, and Bangkok, who came despite the haze and floods.
  • The Oracle Users Group and Oracle ACE teams at Oracle for the generous funding.
  • The local Oracle offices and the School of Engineering, Nanyang Polytechnic for hosting the events including the APEX@University.
  • The event organizers and local Oracle staff that helped run the show.

After first meeting Joel at Kscope12, I remember sharing with him later that there were two goals I wanted to accomplish:

  1. Build an Oracle APEX community for the academic research community.
  2. Grow the Oracle APEX community in Southeast Asia.

Having the very first Oracle APEX roadshow in Indonesia, Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, and Bangkok was a dream come true. Here are some photos of our participants.

First, the full-house event in Jakarta, Indonesia. It amazed me to have almost the same crowd at the end of the day, with many of them remaining after the event to talk to the speakers and take photos.

Oracle APEX events are not new in Singapore, so it was great to be back!

It's hard to compete with the F1 event in Sepang plus an awful amount of haze. I'm grateful to those who showed up.

This was a standing room only event. The Thais were definitely eager to learn, and asked many great questions.

I hope to be back in the region soon to. There's a huge need to evangelize all the benefits of using Oracle Cloud, Oracle AI Database, and Oracle APEX. There's so much to do here to #MOCA, "Make Oracle Cool Again."

It is also my mission to inspire more like-minded individuals in the region to contribute to the technical community in ASEAN. While the Oracle ACE programme can help bring speakers to the event, it is always more beneficial to have local talent to drive these events, speak in local languages and culture.

The Lingering Stain that You Left on Me​

When we concluded our event at the roadshow's final stop in Bangkok, we explained why we always did an "APEX Wave" at our technical conferences. To me, and I'm sure many others, it's our way of remembering the great man Joel was.

Joel, you continue to be my role model. If I could not contribute to the betterment of others like you did, then my life would be in vain.

Elevate and serve others. ~ Joel Kallman [source: https://vimeo.com/556008201]
